## 2.2 Revision of exponent laws (EMAT)

There are several laws we can use to make working with exponential numbers easier. Some of these laws might have
      been done in earlier grades, but we list all the laws here for easy reference:

- \({a}^{m}\times {a}^{n}={a}^{m+n}\)
- \(\dfrac{{a}^{m}}{{a}^{n}}={a}^{m-n}\)
- \({\left(ab\right)}^{n}={a}^{n}{b}^{n}\)
- \({\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)}^{n}=\dfrac{{a}^{n}}{{b}^{n}}\)
- \({\left({a}^{m}\right)}^{n}={a}^{mn}\)

where \(a>0\), \(b>0\) and \(m,n\in \mathbb{R}\)

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 1: Applying the exponential laws">
**Question:**
Simplify:

1. \({2}^{3x}\times {2}^{4x}\)
2. \(\dfrac{4x^{3}}{2x^{5}}\)
3. \(\dfrac{12{p}^{2}{t}^{5}}{3p{t}^{3}}\)
4. \({\left(3x\right)}^{2}\)
5. \({\left({3}^{4}{5}^{2}\right)}^{3}\)
6. \(6p^{0} \times \left(7p\right)^{0}\)
7. \({\left(\dfrac{2xp}{6x^2}\right)}^{3}\)
8. \({\left({2}^{-2}\right)}^{2x+1}\)

1. \({2}^{3x}\times {2}^{4x}={2}^{3x+4x}={2}^{7x}\)
2. \(\dfrac{4x^{3}}{2x^{5}}=2x^{3-5}=2x^{-2}=\dfrac{2}{x^{2}}\)
3. \(\dfrac{12{p}^{2}{t}^{5}}{3p{t}^{3}}=4{p}^{\left(2-1\right)}{t}^{\left(5-3\right)}=4p{t}^{2}\)
4. \({\left(3x\right)}^{2}={3}^{2}{x}^{2}=9{x}^{2}\)
5. \({\left({3}^{4}\times {5}^{2}\right)}^{3}={3}^{\left(4\times 3\right)}\times {5}^{\left(2\times 3\right)}={3}^{12}\times {5}^{6}\)
6. \(6p^{0} \times \left(7p\right)^{0} = 6(1) \times 1 = 6\)
7. \({\left(\dfrac{2xp}{6x^2}\right)}^{3}=\left(\dfrac{p}{3x}\right)^{3}=\dfrac{p^{3}}{27x^{3}}\)
8. \({\left({2}^{-2}\right)}^{2x+1}=2^{-2(2x+1)}=2^{-4x-2}\)
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 2: Exponential expressions">
**Question:**
Simplify: \(\dfrac{{2}^{2n}\times {4}^{n}\times 2}{{16}^{n}}\)

**Change the bases to prime numbers:**
At first glance it appears that we cannot simplify this expression. However, if we reduce the bases to prime
          bases, then we can apply the exponent laws.

        \[\dfrac{{2}^{2n} \times {4}^{n} \times 2}{{16}^{n}} = \dfrac{{2}^{2n} \times {\left({2}^{2}\right)}^{n} \times
        {2}^{1}}{{\left({2}^{4}\right)}^{n}}\]

**Simplify the exponents:**
\begin{align*}
        & =\dfrac{{2}^{2n} \times {2}^{2n} \times {2}^{1}}{{2}^{4n}} \\
        & = \dfrac{{2}^{2n + 2n + 1}}{{2}^{4n}} \\
        & = \dfrac{{2}^{4n + 1}}{{2}^{4n}} \\
        & ={2}^{4n + 1 - \left(4n\right)} \\
        & = 2
        \end{align*}
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 3: Exponential expressions">
**Question:**
Simplify:

        \[\dfrac{{5}^{2x - 1} \cdot {9}^{x - 2}}{{15}^{2x - 3}}\]

**Change the bases to prime numbers:**
\begin{align*}
        \dfrac{{5}^{2x - 1} \cdot {9}^{x - 2}}{{15}^{2x - 3}} & = \dfrac{{5}^{2x - 1} \cdot
        {\left({3}^{2}\right)}^{x - 2}}{{\left(5\times 3\right)}^{2x - 3}} \\
        & = \dfrac{{5}^{2x - 1} \cdot {3}^{2x - 4}}{{5}^{2x - 3} \cdot {3}^{2x - 3}}
        \end{align*}

**Subtract the exponents (same base):**
\begin{align*}
        & = {5}^{\left(2x - 1\right) - \left(2x - 3\right)} \times {3}^{\left(2x - 4\right) - \left(2x - 3\right)}
        \\
        & = {5}^{2x - 1 - 2x + 3} \times {3}^{2x - 4 - 2x + 3} \\
        & = {5}^{2} \times {3}^{-1}
        \end{align*}

**Write the answer as a fraction:**
\begin{align*}
        &= \dfrac{{5}^{2}}{3} \\
        & = \dfrac{25}{3}
        \end{align*}
</Callout>

<Callout type="note" title="Note">
When working with exponents, all the laws of operation for algebra apply.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 4: Simplifying by taking out a common factor">
**Question:**
Simplify:

        \[\dfrac{{2}^{t} - {2}^{t - 2}}{3\cdot {2}^{t} - {2}^{t}}\]

**Simplify to a form that can be factorised:**
For each of the exponent laws we can “undo” the law - in other words we can work backwards. For
          this expression we can reverse the multiplication law to write \(2^{t - 2}\) as \(2^{t} \cdot 2^{-2}\).

        \[\frac{{2}^{t} - {2}^{t - 2}}{3\cdot {2}^{t} - {2}^{t}} = \frac{{2}^{t} - \left({2}^{t}\cdot
        {2}^{-2}\right)}{3\cdot {2}^{t}-{2}^{t}}\]

**Take out a common factor:**
\[= \frac{{2}^{t}\left(1 - {2}^{-2}\right)}{{2}^{t}\left(3 - 1\right)}\]

**Cancel the common factor and simplify:**
\begin{align*}
        & = \frac{1-2^{-2}}{3-1} \\
        & = \frac{1 - \frac{1}{4}}{2}\\
        & = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{2} \\
        & = \frac{3}{8}
        \end{align*}
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 5: Simplifying using difference of two squares">
**Question:**
Simplify:

        \[\dfrac{{9}^{x} - 1}{{3}^{x} + 1}\]

**Change the bases to prime numbers:**
\begin{align*}
        \dfrac{{9}^{x} - 1}{{3}^{x} + 1} & = \dfrac{{\left({3}^{2}\right)}^{x} - 1}{{3}^{x} + 1} \\
        & = \dfrac{{3}^{2x} - 1}{{3}^{x} + 1} \qquad \text{Recognise that } 3^{2x} = \left(3^{x}\right)^{2}
        \end{align*}

**Factorise using the difference of squares:**
\[= \dfrac{\left({3}^{x} - 1\right)\left({3}^{x} + 1\right)}{{3}^{x} + 1}\]

**Cancel the common factor and simplify:**
\[= {3}^{x}-1\]
</Callout>

**Problem:**
\(\dfrac{\text{16}^{x}- \text{144}^{b}}{\text{4}^{x}- \text{12}^{b}}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              \frac{ \text{16} ^{x} - \text{144} ^{b}}{\text{4} ^{x}- \text{12}^{b}} & =
              \frac{{\left(\text{4}^\text{2}\right)}^x- {\left(\text{12}^\text{2}\right)}^b}{\text{4}^{x}-
              \text{12}^{b}} \\
              &= \frac{{\left(\text{4}^x\right)}^{\text{2}}- {\left(\text{12}^b\right)}^{\text{2}}}{\text{4}^{x}-
              \text{12}^{b}} \\
              &= \frac{\left(\text{4}^x- \text{12}^b\right)\left(\text{4}^x+ \text{12}^b\right)}{\text{4}^{x}-
              \text{12}^{b}} \\
              &= \text{4}^{x}+ \text{12}^{b}
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**

**Problem:**
\(\dfrac{9^3\times 20^2}{4\times 5^2 \times 3^5}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
            \frac{9^3\times 20^2}{4\times 5^2 \times 3^5} & = \frac{3^6 4^2 5^2}{4 \times 5^2 3^5} \\
            & = \frac{3^6 2^4 5^2}{2^2 5^2 3^5} \\
            & = 3^{6 - 5} 2^{4 - 2} 5^{2 - 2} \\
            & = 3 \times 2^2 \\
            & = 12
            \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(\dfrac{7^{b} + 7^{b - 2}}{4 \times 7^{b} + 3 \times 7^{b}}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
            \frac{7^{b} + 7^{b - 2}}{4 \times 7^{b} - 3 \times 7^{b}}
            &= \frac{7^{b}(1 + 7^{-2})}{7^{b}(4 - 3)} \\
            &= \frac{(1 + 7^{-2})}{1} \\
            &= \frac{1 + \frac{1}{49}}{1} \\
            & = \frac{51}{49}
            \end{align*}

**Problem:**
