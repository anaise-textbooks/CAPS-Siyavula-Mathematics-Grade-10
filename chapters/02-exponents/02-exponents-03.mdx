## 2.4 Exponential equations (EMAW)

Exponential equations have the unknown variable in the exponent. Here are some examples:

    \begin{align*}
    {3}^{x + 1} & = 9 \\
    {5}^{t} + 3 \times {5}^{t - 1}& = 400
    \end{align*}


If we can write a single term with the same base on each side of the equation, we can equate the exponents. This
      is one method to solve exponential equations.

Important: if \(a>0\) and \(a\ne 1\) then:

    \begin{align*}
    {a}^{x}& ={a}^{y} \\
    \text{then } x& = y \text{ (same base)}
    \end{align*}


Also notice that if \(a = 1\), then \(x\) and \(y\) can be different.

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 8: Equating exponents">
**Question:**
Solve for \(x\): \({3}^{x + 1}=9\).

**Change the bases to prime numbers:**
\[{3}^{x + 1} = {3}^{2}\]

**The bases are the same so we can equate exponents:**
\begin{align*}
        x+1& = 2 \\
        \therefore x & = 1
        \end{align*}
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 9: Equating exponents">
**Question:**
Solve for \(t\): \({3}^{t}=1\).

**Solve for \(t\):**
We know from the exponent identities that \(a^{0}=1\), therefore:

        \begin{align*}
        {3}^{t}& = 1 \\
        3^{t} & = 3^{0} \\
        \therefore t &= 0
        \end{align*}
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 10: Solving equations by taking out a common factor">
**Question:**
Solve for \(t\): \({5}^{t} + 3\cdot {5}^{t + 1} = 400\).

**Rewrite the expression:**
\[{5}^{t} + 3\left({5}^{t} \cdot 5\right) = 400\]

**Take out a common factor:**
\begin{align*}
        {5}^{t}\left(1+3\cdot5\right)&=400 \\
        {5}^{t}\left(1 + 15\right) &= 400
        \end{align*}

**Simplify:**
\begin{align*}
        {5}^{t}\left(16\right) & = 400 \\
        {5}^{t} & =25
        \end{align*}

**Change the bases to prime numbers:**
\[{5}^{t} = {5}^{2}\]

**The bases are the same so we can equate exponents:**
\[\therefore t=2\]
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 11: Solving equations by factorising a trinomial">
**Question:**
Solve for \(x\): \[3^{2x}-80\cdot 3^{x} - 81 = 0\]

**Factorise the trinomial:**
\[(3^{x}-81)(3^{x}+1)=0\]

**Solve for \(x\):**
\(3^x = 81\) or \(3^x = -1\). However \(3^x = -1\) is undefined, so:

        \begin{align*}
        3^{x}& = 81 \\
        {3}^{x} & = 3^{4} \\
        x & = 4
        \end{align*}


Therefore \(x = 4\)
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 12: Solving equations by factorising a trinomial">
**Question:**
Solve for \(p\): \[p-13{p}^{\frac{1}{2}} + 36 = 0\]

**Rewrite the equation:**
We notice that \({\left({p}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}^{2} = p\) so we can rewrite the equation as:

        \[{\left({p}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}^{2} - 13{p}^{\frac{1}{2}} + 36 = 0\]

**Factorise as a trinomial:**
\[\left({p}^{\frac{1}{2}} - 9\right)\left({p}^{\frac{1}{2}} - 4\right) = 0\]

**Solve to find both roots:**
\(\begin{array}{rlcrl}
        {p}^{\frac{1}{2}} - 9& = 0 & \text{ or } & {p}^{\frac{1}{2}} - 4 & =0 \\
        {p}^{\frac{1}{2}} & =9 & & {p}^{\frac{1}{2}} & =4 \\
        {\left({p}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}^{2} & = {\left(9\right)}^{2} & &
        {\left({p}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}^{2} & = {\left(4\right)}^{2} \\
        p & =81 & & p & =16
        \end{array}\)


Therefore \(p = 81\) or \(p = 16\).
</Callout>

Learners may find Worked Example 13 much easier using the \(k\)-substitution method. You may choose to return
        to this example once the \(k\)-substitution has been taught.

The solution using \(k\)-substitution is as follows:

      \begin{align*}
      2^{x}-2^{4-x} &=0 \\
      2^{x}-2^{4} \cdot 2^{-x} & = 0\\
      2^{x}-\dfrac{2^{4}}{2^{x}} & = 0 \\
      \text{Let } 2^{x}&=k\\
      k-\dfrac{2^{4}}{k} &=0\\
      \times k \qquad &k^{2}-16 = 0 \\
      (k-4)(k+4)=0\\
      k = -4 & \text{ or } \qquad k = 4 \\
      2^{x} \ne -4 & \qquad 2^{x}=4 \\
      & \qquad 2^{x}=2^{2}=4 \\
      x& = 2
      \end{align*}


<Callout type="tip" title="Worked example 13: Solving equations by factorisation">
**Question:**
Solve for \(x\): \[2^{x}-2^{4-x} = 0\]

**Rewrite the equation:**
In order to get the equation into a form which we can factorise, we need to rewrite the equation:

        \begin{align*}
        2^{x}-2^{4-x} &= 0 \\
        2^{x}-2^{4} \cdot 2^{-x} &= 0 \\
        2^{x}-\frac{2^{4}}{2^{x}} &= 0
        \end{align*}


Now eliminate the fraction by multiplying both sides of the equation by the denominator, \(2^{x}\).

        \begin{align*}
        \left(2^{x}-\frac{2^{4}}{2^{x}}\right) \times 2^{x}&= 0 \times 2^{x} \\
        2^{2x}-16 &= 0
        \end{align*}

**Factorise the equation:**
Now that we have rearranged the equation, we can see that we are left with a difference of two squares.
          Therefore:

        \begin{align*}
        2^{2x}-16 &= 0 \\
        (2^x - 4)(2^x+4) &=0 \\
        2^x & = 4 \qquad 2^x \ne -4 \quad \text{(a positive integer with an exponent is always positive)}\\
        2^{x}=2^{2} & =4 \\
        x& = 2
        \end{align*}


Therefore \(x = 2\).
</Callout>

### Textbook Exercise 2.3

Solve for the variable:

**Problem:**
\(2^{x + 5} = 32\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              2^{x + 5} & = 32 \\
              2^{x + 5} & = 2^{5} \\
              \therefore x + 5 & = 5 \\
              x & = 0
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(5^{2x + 2} = \dfrac{1}{125}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              5^{2x + 2} & = \dfrac{1}{125} \\
              5^{2x + 2} & = \dfrac{1}{5^{3}} \\
              5^{2x + 2} & = 5^{-3}\\
              \therefore 2x + 2 & = -3 \\
              2x & = -5\\
              x & = -\dfrac{5}{2}
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(64^{y + 1} = 16^{2y + 5}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              64^{y + 1} & = 16^{2y + 5} \\
              2^{6(y + 1)} & = 2^{4(2y + 5)} \\
              2^{6y + 6} & = 2^{8y + 20}\\
              \therefore 6y + 6 & = 8y + 20 \\
              2y & = -14\\
              y & = -7
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(3^{9x - 2} = 27\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              3^{9x - 2} & = 27 \\
              3^{9x - 2} & = 3^{3} \\
              \therefore 9x - 2 & = 3 \\
              9x & = 5\\
              x & = \dfrac{5}{9}
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(\text{25} = \text{5}^ {z -4}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
                \text{25} & = \text{5}^ {z -4} \\
                \text{5}^\text{2} & = \text{5}^ {z -4} \\
                \text{2} & = z -4 \\
                \text{2} +4 & = z \\
                \text{6} & = z
                \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(- \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6^{\frac{m}{2} + 3} = -18\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
                \left( -2 \right) \left( - \frac{1}{2} 6^{\frac{m}{2} + 3} \right) & = \left( -18 \right) \left( -2
                \right) \\
                6^{\frac{m}{2} + 3} & = 36 \\
                6^{\frac{m}{2} + 3} & = 6^{2} \\
                \frac{m}{2} + 3 & = 2 \\
                \frac{m}{2} & = -1 \\
                m & = -2
                \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(81^{k + 2} = 27^{k + 4}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              81^{k + 2} & = 27^{k + 4} \\
              3^{4(k + 2)} & = 3^{3(k + 4)} \\
              \therefore 4k + 8 & = 3k + 12 \\
              k & = 4
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(25^{1 - 2x} - 5^{4} = 0\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              25^{1 - 2x} - 5^{4} & = 0 \\
              5^{2(1 - 2x)} & = 5^{4} \\
              5^{2 - 4x} & = 5^{4} \\
              \therefore 2 - 4x & = 4 \\
              4x & = -2\\
              x & = -\dfrac{1}{2}
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(27^{x} \times 9^{x - 2} = 1\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              27^{x} \times 9^{x - 2} & = 1 \\
              3^{3x} \times 3^{2(x - 2)} & = 1 \\
              3^{3x + 2x - 4} & = 3^{0} \\
              \therefore 5x - 4 & = 0 \\
              5x & = 4\\
              x & = \frac{4}{5}
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(2^{t} + 2^{t + 2} = 40\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              2^{t} + 2^{t + 2} & = 40 \\
              2^{t}(1 + 2^{2}) & = 40\\
              2^{t}(5) & = 40 \\
              2^{t} & = 8 \\
              2^{t} & = 2^{3}\\
              \therefore t & = 3
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\((7^x - 49)(3^x - 27) = 0\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              (7^x - 49)(3^x - 27) &= 0 \\
              (7^x - 7^2)(3^x - 3^3) &= 0 \\
              \therefore 7^x - 7^2 = 0 &\text{ or } 3^x - 3^3 = 0 \\
              \therefore 7^x = 7^2 &\text{ or } 3^x = 3^3 \\
              \therefore x = 2 &\text{ or } x = 3
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\((2 \cdot 2^x - 16)(3^{x+1} - 9) = 0\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              (2 \cdot 2^x - 16)(3^{x+1} - 9) &= 0 \\
              (2^{x+1} - 2^4)(3^{x+1} - 3^2) &= 0 \\
              \therefore 2^{x + 1} - 2^4 = 0 &\text{ or } 3^{x+1} - 3^2 = 0 \\
              x + 1 = 4 &\text{ or } x + 1 = 2 \\
              \therefore x = 3 &\text{ or } x = 1
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\((10^x - 1)(3^x - 81) = 0\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              (10^x - 1)(3^x - 81) &= 0 \\
              (10^x - 10^0)(3^x - 3^4)& = 0 \\
              \therefore 10^x - 10^0 = 0 &\text{ or } 3^x - 3^4 = 0 \\
              \therefore x = 0 &\text{ or } x = 4
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(2 \times 5^{2 - x} = 5 + 5^{x}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              2 \times 5^{2 - x} & = 5 + 5^{x} \\
              2(5^{2})(5^{-x}) & = 5 + 5^{x}\\
              \dfrac{2(5^{2})}{5^{x}} - 5 - 5^{x} & = 0 \\
              \left(\dfrac{50}{5^{x}}\right) \times 5^{x} -5 \times 5^{x} - 5^{x} \times 5^{x} & = 0 \\
              50 - 5(5^{x}) - \left(5^{x}\right)^{2} & = 0 \\
              \left(5^{x} - 5\right)\left(5^{x} + 10\right) & = 0 \\
              5^{x} - 5 = 0 \text{ or } & 5^{x} + 10 = 0\\
              5^{x} = 5 \text{ or } & 5^{x} = -10\\
              x = 1 \text{ or } & \text{ undefined}\\
              \therefore x & = 1
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(9^{m} + 3^{3 - 2m} = 28\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              9^{m} + 3^{3 - 2m} & = 28 \\
              3^{2m} + 3^{3}.3^{-2m} & = 28 \\
              3^{2m} + \frac{27}{3^{2m}} - 28 & = 0 \\
              \left(3^{2m}\right)^{2} - 28\left(3^{2m}\right) + 27 & = 0 \\
              \left(3^{2m} - 27\right)\left(3^{2m} - 1\right) & = 0 \\
              3^{2m} - 27 = 0 \text{ or } & 3^{2m} - 1 = 0\\
              3^{2m} = 3^{3} \text{ or } & 3^{2m} = 3^{0}\\
              2m = 3 \text{ or } & 2m = 0\\
              \therefore m & = \dfrac{3}{2} \text{ or } 0
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(y - 2y^{\frac{1}{2}} + 1 = 0\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              y - 2y^{\frac{1}{2}} + 1 & = 0 \\
              \left(y^{\frac{1}{2}} - 1\right)\left(y^{\frac{1}{2}} - 1\right) & = 0 \\
              y^{\frac{1}{2}} - 1 & = 0\\
              y^{\frac{1}{2}} & = 1\\
              y^{\frac{1}{2} \times 2} & = 1^{1 \times 2}\\
              y & = 1^{2} \\
              \therefore y & = 1
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(4^{x + 3} = \text{0,5}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              4^{x + 3} & = \text{0,5} \\
              2^{2x + 6} & = \dfrac{1}{2} \\
              2^{2x + 6} & = 2^{-1}\\
              \therefore 2x + 6 & = -1 \\
              2x & = -7\\
              x & = -\dfrac{7}{2}
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(2^{a} = \text{0,125}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              2^{a} & = \text{0,125} \\
              2^{a} & = \dfrac{1}{8} \\
              2^{a} & = 2^{-3}\\
              \therefore a & = -3
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(10^{x} = \text{0,001}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              10^{x} & = \text{0,001} \\
              10^{x} & = \dfrac{1}{\text{1 000}} \\
              10^{x} & = 10^{-3}\\
              \therefore x & = -3
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(2^{x^{2} - 2x - 3} = 1\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              2^{x^{2} - 2x - 3} & = 1 \\
              2^{x^{2} - 2x - 3} & = 2^{0} \\
              \therefore x^{2} - 2x - 3 & = 0 \\
              (x - 3)(x + 1) & = 0\\
              \therefore x & = 3 \text{ or } -1
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(\dfrac{8^x - 1}{2^x - 1} = 8.2^x + 9\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              \frac{8^x - 1}{2^x - 1} &= 8.2^x + 9 \\
              \frac{2^{3x} - 1}{2^x - 1} &= 8.2^x + 9 \\
              \frac{(2^{x} - 1)(2^{2x} + 2^x + 1)}{2^x - 1} &= 8.2^x + 9 \\
              2^{2x} + 2^x + 1 &= 8.2^x + 9 \\
              2^{2x} + 2^x &= 8.2^x + 8 \\
              2^x.2^x + 2^x &= 8(2^x + 1) \\
              2^x(2^x + 1) &= 8(2^x + 1) \\
              2^x &= 2^3 \\
              \therefore x &= 3
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
\(\dfrac{27^x - 1}{9^x + 3^x + 1} = -\dfrac{8}{9}\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
              \frac{27^x - 1}{9^x + 3^x + 1} &= -\frac{8}{9} \\
              \frac{3^{3x} - 1}{9^x + 3^x + 1} &= -\frac{8}{9} \\
              \frac{(3^x - 1)(9^x + 3^x + 1)}{9^x + 3^x + 1} &= -\frac{8}{9} \\
              3^x - 1 &= -\frac{8}{9} \\
              3^x &= \frac{1}{9} \\
              3^x &= \frac{1}{3^2} \\
              3^x &= 3^{-2} \\
              \therefore x &= -2
              \end{align*}

**Problem:**
The growth of algae can be modelled by the function \(f(t) = 2^{t}\). Find the value of \(t\) such that
              \(f(t) = 128\).

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}
            f(t) = 2^{t} & = 128 \\
            2^{t} & = 2^{7} \\
            \therefore t & = 7
            \end{align*}

**Problem:**
Use trial and error to find the value of \(x\) correct to 2 decimal places

            \(2^x = 7\)

**Solution:**
\begin{align*}

            \text{so } 2 < &x < 3 \text{ but closer to }3 \\
            \text{Test} \\
            2^{\text{2,9}} &= \text{7,464} \\
            2^{\text{2,8}} &= \text{6,964} \\
            2^{\text{2,81}} &= \text{7,01} \\
            2^{\text{2,805}} &= \text{6,989} \\
            2^{\text{2,809}} &= \text{7,007} \\

            \end{align*}
